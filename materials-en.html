Teaching Materials

These materials help children learn how AI works through hands-on activities. First, children learn the ideas "unplugged," without computers. Then they try real AI and check what they learned.

Unplugged

These lessons need only things found in a classroom, such as paper cups and beads.

Paper Cup AI Reinforcement learning

Each paper cup stands for a game state. The "AI" picks a move by drawing a colored bead from the cup. When it wins, beads are added. When it loses, beads are taken away. After many games, the beads in the cups change, and children can see the AI getting stronger.

Grade: upper elementary, parents and children / Materials: 9 paper cups, colored beads

Neural Network Iris classification

Using data on iris flowers, children play the role of neurons. They give scores and decide the type of flower. When the answer is wrong, they change how they give scores. In this way, they experience how a neural network learns.

Grade: elementary school (workshop)

Diffusion Model Image generationComing soon

Children use pixel art made by the class as training data. With dice and lots, they make new pictures. The new pictures become "average," and rare features disappear. From this, children think about the nature of image-generating AI.

Materials: color tiles, dice, paper cups

Using computers

Children use real AI and check the ideas they learned in the unplugged lessons.

AI in Your Hand (Local LLM) Generative AIComing soon

Children use a chat AI that runs on a small computer (Raspberry Pi) without the internet. They compare it with the AI they usually use, and think about where and how AI works.

Equipment: Raspberry Pi, monitor, keyboard
